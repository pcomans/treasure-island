Run everything with `tools/test.sh` in the devcontainer (see the [README](../../README.md#develop)).
It must pass before anything is pushed to `main`.

Checks for one building, used while working on it (see [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md#what-done-means-for-a-building)):

```sh
tools/godot --fixed-fps 60 --path . --script game/tests/shared/building_fit_test.gd -- --source <key>   # renders: no --headless; --fixed-fps 60 walks faster than the wall clock
tools/godot --path . --resolution 1600x900 --script game/tests/shared/building_shots.gd -- --source <key> --island --out <dir>
tools/build-mac.sh   # Mac build + content audit + launch check
```

The game fills its window at any shape (`window/stretch/aspect="expand"`), but the shared harness (`world_harness.gd`) keeps the base 1440 × 900 frame, so captures stay 1440 × 900 at any `--resolution`.

A `--views` focused view may add `camera_xyz` with a vertical `fov` (default 70) to shoot from a free camera aimed at its `target_xyz`, for example to match a reference photo, or `"projection": "ortho"` with `ortho_size` (vertical extent in metres) for a square-on elevation. A free camera sees as far as the gameplay camera (its 26 km far plane, which holds the distant horizon) unless it adds `far` (metres, from 100 up to that) to see less far.
The grass and small stones (`game/scripts/world/ground_dressing.gd`) are drawn round the camera at runtime from what the ground draws, so they follow any change to the ground-cover map. They are on in every rendered run on Forward+ and off headless and on the web. `--ground-dressing off|low|medium|high` after `--` sets the density for a run, else the project setting `treasure_island/ground_dressing/density`; `.../cards`, `.../grass`, `.../stones` and `.../render_priority` are for timing them apart. A still `--street-views` or `--views` view may add `dressing`, `dressing_parts`, `dressing_params`, `dressing_maps` and `measure_dressing` (see the header of `building_shots.gd`). `shared/ground_dressing_views.json` is a `--street-views` plan of the poses the grass was judged and timed at, each shot off and on.
`shared/kit_gallery.gd -- --plan shared/kit_gallery_cars.json --out <dir>` shoots street-kit items without loading the island: the kit's own meshes and materials on a plain ground under `main.tscn`'s sun and environment, each item at a named detail level (`hero`, `lod0`, `lod1`, `shadow`) from the plan's fixed cameras, in one launch of a few seconds. Use it for modelling rounds on kit items (the cars' plan has the crossover's close views, the levels side by side and every body at every level; `shared/kit_gallery_car_ring.json` rings every body at the hero and near levels, poses the owner's 2026-11-11 screenshots, shows the driver at the wheel and the dirt levels, in paint and in clay under a raking sun); the island views stay `building_shots.gd`'s. `shared/car_island_views.json` is a `--street-views` plan of two cars in the island (beside, standing on, seated at the wheel, the chase camera; run with `--fixed-fps 60 --show-player --street-detail on`). `node tools/check_street_car_meshes.mjs` (in `tools/test.sh`) reads the built car kits for faces with no area, inside out or folded, broken or wedged panel gaps, see-through spots wider than a third of the owner's pixel, painted-over side windows, hidden number plates, glass and lamps drawn in another material at any level, and a hero cabin poking out of its shell, that the normal drawn on each body's side follows the modelled surface (within 5 degrees, at the near and hero levels: stepped or rippled reflections and dents the triangles make fail it), and each body's own shape for a corner whose profile bulges or dips (the lid's rise from the shoulder running past both its ends' values round a corner: a crumpled reflection at a pillar's foot); its allowances are in `KNOWN_ISSUES.md`.
`--street-views FILE` shoots a street-detail area instead of a building: `{"area": "NAME", "views": [...]}` with the same view fields but no building bound, saved as `street-NAME.png`, with street detail on and off by default.
The player avatar is hidden in every shot; `--show-player` keeps it.
A street view may add `moves` (steps of held input actions, each with `ticks`, an optional camera `turn_deg` and an optional `"frames": false` that saves no frames during the step) and `every` to walk, run, fly, fast-drop (`fast_drop`) or turn the stock player and save a frame every few physics ticks (`street-NAME-000.png`, ...), printing one `MOVE_TICK` line per tick with the avatar's state; run it with `--fixed-fps 60` and `--show-player` for gait strips or a landing.
Each `MOVE_TICK` line also names what the player stands on: `floor_body` (the slide's floor collider) and `support` (the first solid straight under its feet, `OBJECT_KEY@y`). A moving view may add `start_xyz` to start the stock player in the air there, at rest, after it settles at `player_xz`: a landing view onto scenery off the walk route, such as the ferry float or the docked boat's roof. A moving view's free camera (`camera_xyz`) follows the player, keeping its offset; with `"camera_fixed": true` it stays where `camera_xyz` puts it, aimed at the target, for frames of something that moves by itself, such as the docked ferry's rocking.
A street-view plan may also plant street-vegetation kit props for its shots, visual only and gone after them: `beds` (`name`, `center_xz`, `size_m`, `ground`: `mulch`, `gravel` or `street_mulch`, and an optional `edge_m` over which its edge fades out unevenly) lays a bed 2 cm over the drawn ground, and `plants` (`group`, `prop`, `variant`, `scale`, `xz`, `yaw`) stands kit props on it, built as the world builds its vegetation; a hedge prop takes a `path` of `[x, z]` points instead of `xz`, and a plant's optional `level` (`lod0`, `lod1` or `far`, the same for its whole group) draws its group at that detail level only, at any distance. A view may then add `show` (a list of plant groups) to draw only those, for a species gallery, and `measure_frames` to time that many drawn frames with them and as many with every group hidden, in alternating blocks, printing `FRAME_TIME` lines with draw calls and primitives (vsync and the frame cap off while it measures; run in the Compatibility renderer for the web's cost).

Street detail (the vegetation, furniture, fences and clutter layers under `PlayableWorld/StreetDetail`) is on in play, the web build and `island_test.gd`, and off in `building_fit_test.gd`.
The furniture layer's poles, posts, cabinets, hydrants, benches and bins, and the clutter layer's parked cars, cones and barricades and its yard props (containers and container walls, shed rows, yard vehicles, fabric sheds, equipment, piles and berms, pole flags; `street_yard_kit.gd`; a heap's collision is the floor the kit lays over its visible mesh, no triangle steeper than 45 degrees and at most 0.3 m above the visible heap at its largest scale, which the validator checks), are solid on their own street-prop physics layer (layer 4): the player collides with it and the camera arm ignores it. A parked car collides as its drawn body: two or three convex pieces from the kit (`car_<body>_hull<n>`, `tools/lib/street-car-kit.mjs` `carHullMeshes`), each within 1.5 cm of the drawn outline with its roof flat out to the rails, which a live car shares while it stands still (frozen), so the player stands on a car's hood, roof or bed as drawn (`headless_car_stance.gd`); while it moves a live car collides as two simple hulls with the same underside (`drivable_car.gd`), so a pile-up stays cheap (`headless_car_pileup.gd`) and a car knocked over lies still on its roof or side; consecutive cars along a curb keep at least 1 m apart, so the player can walk between them. `automated_route_qa.gd`, `island_test.gd` and fits with `--street-detail on` walk among them; the clearance check keeps them off the walked corridors and the walk straight ahead from the spawn. Gameplay-camera street views print `arm_m`, the camera's distance along its arm.
The fences layer's fences (OSM `barrier=fence` ways and the zones' photo-based fence rules, `tools/lib/street-fence-generation.mjs`; slatted chain-link draws its gates standing open, leaves back on the inside, as plain and screened chain-link draw their rules' gates; the leaves are in the generated gate records and the clearance check reads them) are solid on the same street-prop layer: they stop the stock player, the jetpack clears them and the camera arm ignores them. A fence opens where a road or path crosses it, at an OSM gate and where it meets the gameplay mask; the clearance check also floods the ground around every built fence and fails if a fence, with buildings, other fences, street props or the island's edge, shuts walkable ground in.

The vegetation layer's planted trees (`tools/lib/street-vegetation-generation.mjs` and `game/scripts/world/street_vegetation_kit.gd`) are solid only at their trunks, as capsules on the same street-prop layer: they stop the stock player, the camera arm ignores them and the jetpack flies through their crowns; grass clumps, shrubs, flax and hedges have no collision. `tools/validate_godot_world.mjs` checks that every planted row's foot meets the visible ground (never above it, never buried deeper than the trunk runs on below it, and a tree in paving sits in a mulch pit on the paving's plane), and the clearance check counts tree trunks with the street props.
Fences and the building fit: `island_test.gd` checks every scored building with all layers on, fences included, because fences are part of the world a player walks. A real fence may close one side of a building, as a neighbouring building can. The fit then skips that side (`WALK_UP_SKIPPED`, naming the fence) only when `fenced_approaches` in `tools/config/street-detail-zones.json` lists it, with the fence rows, the reason and dated evidence, and every start the fit tries there is blocked by the listed fence, another building or the island's edge. A fence that closes a side without that listing fails the fit and names the fence; at least two sides must still pass. `building_fit_test.gd` keeps the layers off, so each building's own fit never depends on street detail.

`building_shots.gd` takes close-ups and focused views with it off and context, island and overhead views both ways (`NAME-street-on.png`, `NAME-street-off.png`); `--street-detail off|on|both` sets every view.
Override a test with `-- --street-detail on|off`, or choose layers with `--street-detail-layers vegetation,fences` (`island_test.gd`); the play default is the project setting `treasure_island/street_detail/enabled` and `.../layers`.

`shared/` holds these checks and their helpers (`world_harness.gd`, `building_fit.gd`,
`catalog.gd`, and `vehicle_spots.gd`: the storage-yard bodies the vehicle tests drive, read from `drivable_car.gd`, and where they stand the player to get in). `support/` files are loaded by game scripts at runtime.
