## Authoritative comparison

The primary reference is the [National Park Service NRHP nomination
`08000081`](https://npgallery.nps.gov/NRHP/GetAsset/NRHP/08000081_text),
Section 7, pp. 5–7. It identifies the exterior as a truncated U-shaped,
four-story reinforced-concrete building with a central stepped four-story
section, two three-story wings surrounded by two-story elements, eleven
two-story 18-light windows, a cantilevered canopy and projecting first floor,
three triple-door groups, `8` upper / `7` lower windows per wing, projecting
ends with paired tall windows and a relief composition, and an octagonal control
tower on a wider railed platform under a pyramidal roof. The official [Treasure
Island Museum history](https://www.treasureislandmuseum.org/youarehere/building-the-bay-bridge-&-treasure-island)
and [sculpture context](https://www.treasureislandmuseum.org/sculptures-and-island)
corroborate the site/Building 1 context but do not add surveyed game dimensions.

Direct visual matches that count for `r16681702`:

- the concave truncated-U public front and differentiated lower wings versus
  taller stepped center;
- the dominant sequence of eleven tall multipane center bays;
- the long horizontal entrance canopy/projecting base and three dark entrance
  groups;
- distinct two-level wing and paired-tall-window end families.

The abstract red-brown end plaques do **not** reproduce the documented figure
holding an airplane and are not counted as recognition evidence. Exact story
heights, facade offsets, roof pitch, glass products, ornament, and rear cadence
remain production inference or unclaimed.

For `w1222720021`, the NPS description supports an octagonal glazed control tower,
wider viewing platform, metal balustrade, south-side stair, raised-seam pyramidal
roof, mixed/divided-light windows, and rib/flute details. The current diagnostic
shows the broad body/platform/roof family, but the ordinary views do not expose
enough of those cues at a useful scale for a separate top-1 decision.
