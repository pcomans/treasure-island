## Authoritative comparison and truth boundary

The primary authority is the [National Park Service NRHP record and nomination
`08000083`](https://npgallery.nps.gov/AssetDetail/NRIS/08000083), not the frozen
OSM `08000081` value. NPS identifies the Palace of Fine and Decorative Arts /
Building 3 as a 1938 Art Moderne reinforced-concrete hangar. Section 7 describes
an open three-hinge riveted steel arch-truss system, a `335 ft × 225 ft` original
hangar, an `80 ft` overall height, and four tapered concrete corner pylons with
low pyramidal tops. The current P1 packet narrows the authorized ENE appearance
to one very large central sliding-door field with complete pale surround, one
long shallow upper band, and the broad crown/shoulder silhouette.

Direct visible matches that count:

- the broad curved/barrel crown and narrow raised corner-shoulder family;
- one, not two, dominant central hangar opening bounded by a complete surround;
- the single subordinate band below the crown.

The narrow dark groove that makes the band readable is treated only as a
reversible depth-read inference, not observed dark paint. The band is a third
cue, so acceptance does not depend on that inference: the structural silhouette
and one-opening composition already satisfy the two-cue minimum.

The current long sides, WSW end, minor service elements, exact pylon section,
door and band dimensions, roof assembly, interiors, and transformed game-space
vertical values are not established by these views. They remain unclaimed. No
external source photograph is included in the packet.
