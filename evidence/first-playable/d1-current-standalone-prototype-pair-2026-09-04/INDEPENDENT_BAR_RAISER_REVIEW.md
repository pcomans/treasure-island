## Pixel-first method and evidence limitation

I opened all nine `1440x900` PNGs at original detail and fixed the visual read
before consulting the manifest, README, config metadata, source packets, or
confusion packets. I judged only the rendered architecture and ignored the
technical claim cards.

The nominal originals contain large embedded header cards with the target IDs
and claimed cue descriptions. That prevents a literally label-free blind read.
The verdict therefore treats the image region below each card as the visual
evidence and uses no card text as proof. Future packets should provide clean
originals plus separate annotated copies.

Image-only read:

- **B201:** an exceptionally long, one-storey light bar with a continuous dark
  upper register, strong pale piers, three horizontal edge bands, a long real-
  depth canopy, secondary posts, and a mostly quiet lower field. The canopy and
  sparse lower leaves interrupt the long rhythm enough to make this a whole
  elevation rather than a single repeated tile. The same depth/value hierarchy
  survives the reversed-light view.
- **B225:** a low compact bar with a near-roof band of small dark panes in pale
  frames over a horizontally lined lower field. The clerestory groups vary,
  have real recess, and remain legible under reversed light. The lower wall,
  however, reads as fourteen dark, perfectly parallel full-width rules over a
  flat swatch. In the close and oblique views it approaches a barcode or ruled
  block rather than aged horizontal cladding.
- **Pair overview:** the two facade families are immediately different even
  without IDs or map location. B201 reads long/open-above/solid-below with a
  canopy; B225 reads short/clerestory-above/coursed-below. This is useful
  discrimination evidence, although the illustrative pair spacing is not live
  placement evidence.

## B201 — PASS for one reversible live A/B

### What is source-supported and visible

The D1 packet identifies a long WSW-side pair:

1. a nearly continuous upper register of broad dark windows in pale frames,
   divided by substantial light piers and bounded by saturated green bands;
2. a mostly solid warm light lower field with sparse openings across an
   unusually long, low one-storey mass; and
3. a green parapet/canopy hierarchy with muted rust-brown posts as supporting
   structure.

All three relationships are visible. More importantly, the first two continue
to read as an open-above/solid-below structural organization if the green hue is
mentally removed. The windows are complete framed/recessed modules, the canopy
has a slab, fascia, underside shadow, and posts, and the lower field remains
appropriately quiet. The changed-light image preserves the hierarchy, so the
result is not dependent on a flattering highlight.

The eighteen equal inferred bays are visibly economical, but they do not alone
constitute procedural wallpaper: the continuous building-scale register is the
observed cue, while canopy extent, posts, sparse leaves, substantial piers, and
the unusually long mass create the larger composition. This passes the current
prototype bar, not an as-built cadence claim.

### Frozen-decoy discrimination

B201 remains top-1 from architecture rather than labels:

- B202 `w34313546` is a constrained exposed-end relationship of one paired
  high opening group, offset door, and canopy-free teal edge—not a 115 m broad
  upper register and canopy.
- Station 48 `w764313741` has small high horizontal windows over a quiet pale
  service field, without B201's broad upper bays and long canopy hierarchy.
- B225 `w95934119` uses a small-pane clerestory over horizontal cladding, not an
  open broad register over a sparse solid lower field.
- B258 `w34313512` uses a historical two-tier tall-window/transom and
  siding/ledge organization, not B201's one upper register.

Green is a supporting source observation, not the deciding cue.

### Ranked visual guidance

1. **Keep the whole-side hierarchy unchanged.** Preserve the broad upper
   register, substantial piers, open-above/solid-below split, central canopy
   depth, and sparse lower field. These are the recognition payload.
2. **Do not freeze the 18-bay cadence as surveyed truth.** Keep it explicitly
   inferred and preserve complete bays across generated run boundaries. If a
   future rights-cleared elevation supports endpoint or grouping exceptions,
   use those rather than random variation.
3. **Finish or omit each inferred lower leaf as a complete shallow assembly.**
   A leaf should have a restrained frame/recess/threshold contact, not become a
   floating dark token. Do not promote any leaf to an entrance or tenant claim.
4. **Tune in the live context, not against the gray test void.** Preserve the
   green value hierarchy under sun and shadow, but reject a fluorescent edge or
   featureless-black glazing if the island lighting makes hue carry the read.

Items 2–4 are bounded integration/final-review guidance, not reasons to hold the
first live A/B.

### Exact integration recommendation

**Proceed to author one new reversible receiver-relative adapter**, limited to
`building:w34313545:wall`, source edge `0`, ordered runs `0..9`, total exact
chain length `115.512661489 m`. Do not instantiate the standalone pair scene or
factory directly from a live builder/registry.

The adapter must derive one cumulative metre coordinate across the complete
ordered chain, preserve complete modules and explicit corner clearance, and
must not restart cadence at any run boundary or infer it from generated U.
Decorative geometry must remain render-only with zero collision, navigation,
and spray ownership. ENE, SSE, NNW, short facets, corners, returns, roof, signs,
and tenant/address claims stay protected.

After attachment, capture an ordinary full observed-side view, a closer
grounded canopy/register oblique, a changed-light view, and one nearby-decoy
context. Only that new live packet can decide whole-object credit.

## B225 — FAIL; repair standalone before integration

### What works

The D1 packet supports a low one-storey NNW side with:

1. a nearly continuous high clerestory of many small panes in pale frames,
   organized as varied complete groups; and
2. aged light-gray/off-white horizontal cladding with directional courses.

The first cue succeeds. Ten groups vary in overall width and use four, five, or
six panes; pale outer frames and mullions project ahead of recessed opaque
glazing. The single near-roof clerestory tier and 5 m low mass survive ordinary
and reversed light. This is a real facade-family cue, not a row of decals.

The horizontal direction of the second cue exists, so the bounded NNW side can
still be distinguished from the frozen decoys. But its **aged cladding** quality
does not. The material is a uniform flat albedo and the visible articulation is
fourteen identical dark reveals, each spanning the full `46.262 m`. There is no
quiet streaking, uneven repainting, peeling/value drift, or other nonperiodic
field behavior supported by the packet.

### Why the combined bar fails

At the whole-side distance, the lower elevation becomes a stack of ruler lines.
At close and oblique distance, the perfectly continuous dark course edges are
more prominent than the cladding field itself. Under changed light they remain
a high-frequency graphic pattern. This is the wrong kind of visual interest:
it makes the object busier but less material, and risks aliasing or shimmer once
it moves through a real player camera.

The clerestory has modest group variation, but uniformly near-black panes and
equal inter-group gaps compress it back toward one repeated band at distance.
That is a correctable secondary limitation. The decisive failure is the
barcode-like lower field, which is one half of the required recognition pair.

The honest absence of lower openings is **not** a failure. The source view
occludes them, so the prototype correctly avoids inventing entries, doors, or
service programs.

### Frozen-decoy discrimination

The recognition skeleton remains top-1 within the bounded observed-side set:

- B201 has broad green-bounded upper bays and a canopy over a sparse solid
  field, not a small-pane clerestory over horizontal cladding.
- B202 has one paired high-opening/door relationship rather than a continuous
  clerestory.
- Historical B258 has two opening tiers with tall windows/transoms and an
  intermediate ledge on a two-storey mass, not one high small-pane register on
  a low 5 m mass.
- Other generic low warehouses remain the hardest decoys; the varied complete
  clerestory is the useful separator, while the current ruled field is too
  generic to add confidence.

That is enough for a promising recognition study, not enough for live art.

### Smallest truthful repair, ranked

1. **Turn the dark rules back into cladding joints.** Reduce reveal darkness,
   apparent thickness, and uninterrupted graphic dominance so the light wall
   remains the field. Preserve horizontal direction and shallow real depth;
   do not invent a new substrate or surveyed course count.
2. **Add quiet, nonperiodic age at material scale.** Use restrained albedo and
   roughness drift suggesting streaking/uneven repainting, with no letters,
   signs, semantic stains, baked light, repeated damage token, or facade-photo
   content. One low-frequency field should cross the entire observed chain.
3. **Protect clerestory group readability.** Keep the varied complete group
   widths and pane counts, but let major group dividers survive at ordinary
   distance. If pane values vary, use only sparse nonsemantic variation and no
   repeated missing/dark-pane signature.
4. **Run a short motion and changed-light check.** Reject the revision if the
   course family produces moire, shimmer, crawling highlights, or a visible
   four-run phase reset.

Do not add lower openings, endpoint features, signs, roof equipment, or damage
to make the wall interesting. Those remain unsupported.

### Exact integration recommendation

**Do not attach the current B225 prototype.** Revise only its standalone NNW
field and recapture the same whole, oblique, close, changed-light, and short
motion evidence. If the bar then passes, a separate reversible adapter may be
authored only for `building:w95934119:wall`, source edge `3`, ordered runs
`10..13`, total exact chain length `46.261968372 m`.

That eventual adapter must use one cumulative metre coordinate for both module
placement and any directional material UV across the full chain. It must not
restart courses, aging, or clerestory phase per run. WSW openings, SSE, ENE,
lower openings, endpoints, corners, returns, and roof equipment stay protected.
