# Treasure Island Island-Scale Evidence

## Status and scope

This is an approach-neutral discovery brief for a private hobby game. **2026-08-27** is the check date for contextual research in this document, not the map-layout cutoff. “Present day” means a broadly contemporary setting, not survey-grade accuracy to that date. The approved playable world is **Treasure Island proper only**: the filled island, its terrain and shoreline, full road/path network, public-space and land-use features, and simple exterior massing for every building footprint in the approved layout snapshot. “Complete” here means island-scale exterior/world coverage; it does **not** imply apartments, rooms, floor plans, interiors, bespoke façades, or individually researched roof forms.

The adjoining natural island and Bay Bridge approaches are outside the playable world. A simple later game boundary will keep the player on Treasure Island at the southern connection. The **San Francisco skyline will appear as a non-playable billboard background**, while **Yerba Buena Island and the Bay Bridge will appear as simple non-playable polygonal scenery**.

This brief supports three approved outcomes: complete island-scale exterior coverage, owner recognition of Treasure Island, and the current milestone's third-person walk/run plus one predefined spray tag. Cars are deferred. One narrow source decision is approved: the map layout will be frozen from the latest raw OpenStreetMap extract available at acquisition/build time. This does not select an engine, import pipeline, rendering method, world scale, route implementation, simple-massing implementation, or the reversible defaults for camera, avatar, color, water, and tag-limit details.

Evidence labels used below:

- **Verified fact** — stated or directly recorded by a cited primary or authoritative source.
- **Inference** — a reasoned interpretation of verified facts; it still needs testing.
- **Uncertainty** — a material layout attribute or contextual claim that the available evidence does not establish.
- **Candidate recognition cue** — a hypothesis about what may make the place recognizable; it is not an approved requirement.

## Approved map-layout baseline — before → after

- **Before:** discovery was trying to reconcile DataSF, TIDA, transit, shoreline, building, and imagery evidence into a literal **2026-08-27** island layout.
- **After (approved):** at acquisition/build time, freeze the latest available **raw OpenStreetMap vector extract** for Treasure Island. All Treasure Island layout features in that frozen snapshot define the coverage inventory: shoreline/terrain-related geometry where present, roads and paths, public-space/land-use features, and exterior building footprints.
- **Consequence:** do not chase whether that OSM snapshot exactly matches a particular day in changing reality, and do not use other sources to add, delete, or move layout features merely to reconcile it to TIDA/DataSF. Other evidence may explain missing attribute types—especially building massing, materials, landmark character, and recognition—but not replace the approved layout baseline.
- **Freeze control:** record the extract/acquisition timestamp and bounds when the later snapshot is actually obtained. No extract was downloaded during this discovery revision.

## Approved current milestone — before → after

- **Before:** roaming perspective, vehicle timing, traffic/access fidelity, spray-content scope, and outdoor traversal restrictions were open.
- **After (approved):** the current milestone is **third-person walking/running** across the island, with **one predefined spray tag**. Cars and vehicle-route behavior are deferred.
- **Traversal rule:** outdoor land space encoded in the frozen OSM snapshot is generally traversable. Do not research, identify, or reproduce real fences, ownership lines, private-access restrictions, or vehicle-access rules. Only intentional game/playability boundaries may later restrict movement.
- **Target and controls:** the milestone targets the owner's MacBook Pro (M1 Pro, 10-core, 32 GB memory) with mouse and keyboard. No device identifier or other private system detail is needed.
- **Low-risk defaults:** detailed camera, avatar, color, water-edge behavior, and tag-limit handling are reversible later defaults, not owner approval gates or island-evidence research requests.
- **World consequence:** retain every snapshot road/path and public-space/land-use feature as part of full island coverage even though cars are not in this milestone. The Treasure Island playable edge and any later gameplay boundaries are separate from the approved non-playable San Francisco billboard and simple YBI/Bay Bridge polygon scenery.

## Approved horizon context — before → after

- **Before:** all off-island skyline, bridge, hill, and adjoining-island treatments were optional and unspecified.
- **After (approved, KISS):** San Francisco appears as a **non-playable billboard background**; Yerba Buena Island and the Bay Bridge appear as **simple non-playable polygonal scenery**. These choices provide distant context without expanding the Treasure Island playable world.
- **Default boundary:** exact shoreline response and southern-boundary presentation are reversible game defaults, not owner questions and not attempts to reproduce real access controls.

## Approved coverage interpretation

The following is a scope interpretation, not a technical approach:

- **Terrain and shoreline:** include the complete Treasure Island landform and water-edge geometry encoded in the frozen OSM snapshot. Any absent elevation/terrain attribute remains a separate evidence or placeholder issue; it does not trigger a currentness reconciliation. Player behavior at the shoreline can use a simple reversible game default.
- **Outdoor traversal:** account for every Treasure Island road/path and other outdoor land feature in the frozen OSM snapshot and treat the outdoor space as generally walkable/runnable. Real fences, private-access boundaries, and vehicle rules are deliberately not modeled or researched; later restrictions must be game/playability boundaries.
- **Buildings:** give every exterior building footprint in the frozen OSM snapshot a simple exterior mass. OSM height/level tags or coarse contextual evidence may inform a mass when convenient, but individualized height, roof, façade, apartment, room, unit-plan, or interior research is not required for this milestone.
- **Public spaces and land use:** account for every Treasure Island park, plaza, promenade/landing, civic ground, and other public-space/land-use feature encoded in the frozen snapshot. Supplemental sources may explain appearance or missing attributes but do not change snapshot membership.
- **Recognition cues:** the cue hypotheses below prioritize verification and distinctiveness inside complete coverage; they cannot be used to shrink the island, road, or building inventory.
- **Spray:** the milestone uses one predefined tag. Any sprayable/non-sprayable distinction is a later game rule applied to simple exterior surfaces, not a real-world access, ownership, or permission classification.

## Evidence summary

### Boundary, shape, topography, and shoreline

**Verified facts**

- Treasure Island is a human-made island built by hydraulic fill in 1936–1937. USGS describes the original island as about **5,500 feet long by 3,400 feet wide and 397 acres**; the San Francisco Area Plan describes its land as entirely level and filled. These are stable origin and gross-form facts, not a current shoreline survey. [S01, S02]
- The island was laid out for an airport and then used for the 1939–1940 Golden Gate International Exposition. The large, regular footprint and level ground therefore predate the current redevelopment. [S01, S02]
- USGS records that the fill was placed behind a perimeter riprap dike. This verifies the constructed shoreline's origin, not that every 2026 shoreline segment still exposes riprap. [S02]
- The current west waterfront includes the completed first phase of Cityside Park. TIDA accepted it for public park use in July 2025, including the **Canopy of Sky** sculpture; a January 2026 city document lists Cityside Park 1 & 2, 5.7 acres, as open. [S08, S09]
- The official DataSF shoreline layer includes San Francisco's islands, but its rows were last updated **2016-07-12** and its publishing status is “Not updated (historical only).” It is not current evidence for a construction-sensitive 2026 water edge. [S15]
- DataSF's five-foot elevation contours were last updated **2023-10-17** and are also marked historical-only. They can corroborate broad low relief, not curbs, berms, new park grades, or construction pads. [S14]

**Inferences**

- From the USGS dimensions and official plan maps, Treasure Island reads as a broad, low, rectilinear/quadrilateral landform, diagonally oriented in the Bay, with a comparatively narrow southern connection and Clipper Cove shaping the south edge. This shape description is an interpretation of maps, not a surveyed 2026 polygon.
- Because the land is nearly level, long sightlines, the water horizon, distant skyline/bridge views, and building masses are likely to carry more orientation value than terrain relief.
- A generic hilly “San Francisco island” would contradict one of Treasure Island's strongest stable geographic traits.
- The approved non-playable San Francisco billboard can reinforce westward orientation, while simple YBI and Bay Bridge polygons can reinforce the southern connection without expanding playable coverage.

**Uncertainties**

- The exact literal 2026 shoreline polyline, including finished park edges and newly raised or armored segments. This no longer gates the layout: the frozen OSM shoreline/landform geometry will be used as captured.
- The exact visual presentation of the simple game boundary at the southern connection and the shoreline response. These are reversible implementation defaults, not owner approval gates or real-access research tasks.

**Candidate recognition cues**

- The flat, man-made island silhouette surrounded by open water.
- The southwest/south indentation and arrival neck rather than a simple rectangle.
- A mixture of hard shoreline edges and landscaped west-waterfront character, where matching snapshot features need appearance attributes.

### Street and path layout character

**Verified facts**

- DataSF's official street-centerline dataset refreshes daily. At the research baseline its source timestamp was **2026-08-27 03:54** and its portal load timestamp was **2026-08-27 10:29**. It distinguishes active/retired segments, City acceptance, one-way status, class, and named intersections. [S03]
- A reproducibility query using a deliberately rough Treasure Island envelope returned **241 active centerline segments and 69 distinct street names**: 142 active legacy `STREETS_TI` segments and 99 newer generic `STREETS` segments. The same envelope contained 70 retired `STREETS_TI` segments. These counts are a useful completeness check, not an approved boundary survey and not proof that every active record was physically built or open. [S03]
- The street schema distinguishes active/retired segments, named corridors, pedestrian links, and other administrative feature classes. These classifications describe the source, not milestone traversal restrictions. [S03]
- The active Treasure Island records show two overlapping street vocabularies:

  - a legacy numbered-and-lettered grid, including 3rd, 4th, 5th, 6th, 8th through 14th TI Streets; Avenues B, D, E, F, G, H, I, M, and N; California Avenue; Avenue of the Palms; and the Gateview/Northpoint/Bayside/Westside residential loops;
  - a newer southwest network including Treasure Island Road, Clipper Cove Avenue, Trade Winds Avenue, Seven Seas Avenue, Bruton Street, Cravath Street, Johnson Street, and Garden Walk. [S03]
- Recorded centerlines form a connected southwest set: Treasure Island Road between Macalla Road, Clipper Cove Avenue, and Trade Winds Avenue; Clipper Cove Avenue and Trade Winds Avenue between Treasure Island Road and Seven Seas Avenue; Seven Seas Avenue through the Bruton/Cravath/Johnson area; and portions of Avenue of the Palms and its small cross-street grid. [S03]
- Contextual official sources do not use one suffix consistently: the DataSF centerline is **Clipper Cove Avenue**, while TIDA material uses **Clipper Cove Way**. This brief treats them as the same corridor for comparison; a missing OSM label would need a transparent fallback, not an on-site currentness chase. [S03, S05]
- The centerline dataset contains an active feature named **Perimeter Path**, adding a distinct perimeter-route cue to the contextual street vocabulary. [S03]

**Inferences**

- The frozen OSM snapshot, not the DataSF count, defines complete road/path layout coverage. DataSF remains useful for understanding the letter/number naming rhythm and local spatial relationships when OSM labels are sparse; it does not add, delete, or move baseline corridors.
- The connected street/path pattern supports several plausible third-person walk/run loops. The approved traversal rule makes real-world street or vehicle access irrelevant to this milestone.
- The contrast between the curving northern residential loops, the orthogonal legacy grid, and the newer southwest streets may help the island feel spatially specific even before individual buildings are recognized.
- Avenue of the Palms appears to function as both a named landmark axis and a connector between the south/west arrival area and the interior grid.

**Uncertainties**

- How many road/path features and which presentation-relevant tags will exist in the later frozen OSM extract. This is an acquisition-time feature inventory, not an access audit.
- Which road names and suffixes need a transparent fallback when OSM omits or conflicts internally. DataSF's Clipper Cove Avenue/Way discrepancy is contextual evidence, not a reason to reconcile the frozen geometry to a particular date.

**Candidate recognition cues**

- The recognizable street-name rhythm: numbered TI Streets crossing lettered Avenues.
- Avenue of the Palms as a visual axis.
- The Clipper Cove–Seven Seas–Trade Winds southwest loop contrasted with the older grid and northern residential loops.

### Exterior building coverage and “blueprint shape” evidence

**Verified facts**

- No user-supplied island-scale footprint, CAD, GIS, or other shape file was supplied in this project directory as checked on **2026-08-27**. None is required for the approved baseline because the frozen OSM snapshot defines exterior-footprint coverage.
- The closest official public evidence to “house blueprint shapes” is DataSF's **Building Footprints** dataset. It contains exterior multipolygons and LiDAR-derived ground, return-surface, and height summary fields. It is **not** a collection of architectural blueprints, apartments, rooms, floor plans, or interiors. [S13]
- DataSF states that the footprint extents were collapsed from a **2010 Pictometry 3D building model** and later split into likely individual structures using parcel lines and existing mass vertices. A rough Treasure Island envelope query returned **549 footprint records**; every returned `sf16_bldgid` began with the `201006` epoch, and every returned row carried `data_as_of=2023-09-11`. The portal's August 2026 refresh therefore does not make the underlying island geometry a 2026 capture. The rough-envelope count is a reproducibility check, not an authoritative present-day building count. [S13]
- The same footprint records expose height statistics, including median and maximum height-surface values, but their presence does not make demolished 2010 buildings current or add buildings completed after 2010. The metadata does not establish a new 2026 height capture for Treasure Island. [S13]
- DataSF's **Updated Treasure Island Addresses and Building Numbers** is a 473-row tabular list with building numbers, legacy/current-address fields, and limited common names. Its rows were last updated **2016-08-11**, and it contains no geometry column. It can cross-check legacy identifiers but cannot supply exterior shapes or a 2026 inventory. [S21]
- Current TIDA/City documents establish the existence, site/block, completion state, and coarse storey form of several post-2010 buildings, but they do not provide one complete as-built exterior-footprint layer. Likewise, assessor block maps describe parcels rather than verified building outlines, and redevelopment plans mix current, future, and illustrative forms. [S05, S07, S11, S22]

**Inferences**

- The 2010 DataSF layer is useful historical/massing context, but it is no longer a candidate for adding, removing, or relocating footprint geometry relative to the approved OSM snapshot.
- Complete coverage requires one simple exterior mass for every OSM building footprint. Missing height or roof tags do not block coverage: a consistent coarse/default mass is acceptable, while optional evidence may refine a few recognition-critical silhouettes without changing footprint geometry.
- Representative typology samples may help keep the simple masses visually coherent, but individualized building research is not required. This is a hobby-scale consistency hypothesis, not permission to omit snapshot features.

**Uncertainties**

- How many Treasure Island building footprints the later frozen OSM extract contains and which optional `height`, `building:levels`, roof, or part tags are available for convenient coarse variation.
- Whether a small number of recognition-critical silhouettes benefit enough from optional contextual height evidence to justify departing from the general simple-massing rule.
- How OSM classifies small utility buildings, sheds, roofed shelters, construction trailers, and other borderline structures in the frozen snapshot; complete coverage follows snapshot membership rather than creating a separate real-world completeness chase.

#### Approved $0 layout baseline: raw OpenStreetMap vectors

**Verified facts**

- OpenStreetMap makes raw map data available for download in formats such as OSM XML, including small-area exports and filtered queries. OSM describes its map data as open data under the ODbL, with attribution requirements. [S23]
- OSM's data model can encode roads and trails, building outlines/parts, land use, parks and other public-space features. Its Simple 3D Buildings documentation also permits optional `height`, `building:levels`, material, colour, and roof tags. The schema's ability to hold those tags does not establish that Treasure Island objects actually have them. [S23]
- Raw OSM vector data and the public raster tiles at `tile.openstreetmap.org` are different things. The OSM Foundation says the data is free to use but its community-funded tile servers are limited, best-effort, and prohibit bulk/offline tile scraping. The public tile service is therefore not equivalent to a downloadable world-asset source. [S23]
- No OSM Treasure Island extract was downloaded in this discovery pass. The actual snapshot feature counts and missing-attribute counts therefore remain unknown until acquisition/build time; no comparison against a literal 2026 real-world layout is required.

**Approved decision and limitation**

- Raw OSM vectors are the approved **$0 layout baseline** for full-island shoreline/landform geometry where present, roads and paths, exterior building footprints, land-use context, and public-space outlines. The latest extract available when acquired will be frozen rather than reconciled against DataSF/TIDA or a literal date in the changing real world.
- OSM is not presumed to contain terrain elevation, reliable height/roof attributes for every building, façade detail, or materials. Missing height and roof tags do not block the approved simple exterior massing; appearance and any optional landmark refinement remain separate questions. This decision does not select the download mechanism, conversion/import pipeline, engine, or rendering approach.

### Distinctive buildings, landmarks, and current built form

**Verified facts**

- Historic Buildings **1, 2, and 3** stand on Treasure Island's south side and were built for the Golden Gate International Exposition. They later served Pan American Airways as the terminal and two airplane hangars for trans-Pacific Clipper service. [S01, S17]
- The February 2026 TIDA Director's Report says the Treasure Island Museum's free public gallery in **Building One** remains open as a visitor center and was expanding into a second gallery overlooking Clipper Cove and Bay Bridge views. [S10]
- TIDA's April 2025 construction update describes Stage 2 work around Buildings 2 and 3, corroborating that both hangars remained active physical landmarks during the redevelopment context recorded by that source. [S05]
- A new passenger ferry dock opened in 2022 and interim service began between Treasure Island and downtown San Francisco. It adds a visible waterfront arrival element near the south/west civic cluster. [S12]
- The current southwest skyline is not represented by the old building-footprint layer. Official records establish five substantial post-2010 Treasure Island masses:

  - **Maceo May** is a six-story structure completed in January 2023;
  - **Star View Court** was completed in May 2024; published sources differ between six and seven stories, so any supplemental coarse-height value must label that discrepancy;
  - **Isle House** has a seven-level podium and 22-story tower component and was completed in December 2024;
  - **Hawkins** is a six-story building completed in August 2025;
  - **490 Avenue of the Palms** is a six-story building that received temporary occupancy on 2025-10-07. [S11, S22]
- Those sources support island-scale exterior massing only. No Hawkins apartment, unit, room, floor-plan, or interior material is used or needed in this brief.
- Cityside Park Phase 1 is a current west-waterfront landmark and includes Canopy of Sky. Official planning/arts material locates the park against views of downtown San Francisco, the Bay Bridge, and the Golden Gate Bridge. [S08, S18]

**Inferences**

- The monumental south-side exposition ensemble—Building 1 plus the two hangars—likely carries more place-recognition value than any single ordinary residential structure.
- The juxtaposition of exposition/Navy-era large buildings, low-rise neighborhoods, newer large housing masses, park landscape, and construction character is probably a useful evolving-island cue. A uniformly historic or uniformly completed-future treatment would miss this transition.
- The ferry landing, Building 1, Clipper Cove, and the hangars form a compact arrival ensemble that may communicate both island geography and history in one view sequence.

**Uncertainties**

- Which optional OSM height, level, part, or roof attributes can cheaply improve a few recognition-critical simple masses; missing attributes do not block the default massing rule.
- Exact façade colors, cladding, glazing, weathering, signage, lighting, doors, landscaping, and ground materials as seen from the approved third-person walk/run view.
- Whether and how Bay FC, water-resource, or other construction character should influence the appearance of snapshot features; it does not alter snapshot layout membership.
- Which broad simple exterior placements keep the one predefined spray tag legible. This is a game-readability question only, not a classification of real ownership, access, or permission.

**Candidate recognition cues**

- Building 1's civic/terminal mass paired with the enormous Hangars 2 and 3.
- The ferry pier and Clipper Cove water beside the south-side ensemble.
- Isle House as the tallest completed current residential marker, with Maceo May, Star View Court, Hawkins, and 490 Avenue of the Palms producing lower companion masses.
- Cityside Park and Canopy of Sky as a redeveloped west-waterfront cue.
- The visible old-base/new-neighborhood transition as an appearance hypothesis.

The table also repeats the name “Treasure Island Landing” with two acreages, so its inventory needs reconciliation rather than literal reuse. [S09]
- A current RPD page says the department would begin managing TI/YBI parks on 2026-07-01 and that parks not yet renovated remain under TIDA. Its future tense remained on the page after that date, so it does not independently prove the handoff occurred on schedule. [S24]
- Earlier TIDA governance material identifies Waterfront/Ferry Plaza, Building 1 Plaza, Marina Plaza, and Clipper Cove Promenade as major civic/public-realm pieces around the south arrival ensemble. That 2023 material is now relevant only to the character of matching frozen-snapshot features, not their membership or geometry. [S25]

**Inferences**

- The frozen OSM snapshot defines which public-space and land-use features belong to complete layout coverage. The official evidence here can explain the likely gross character of matching Cityside, landing/ferry, Building 1–Clipper Cove, lawn, promenade, or park features; it does not determine snapshot membership.
- Representing each snapshot space's gross outline, dominant circulation, large lawn/plaza/planting/water relationships, and any unique large artwork can satisfy island-scale coverage without implying bespoke modeling of every bench, planter, or sign. The exact feature threshold remains a later fidelity choice.

**Uncertainties**

- Which park, plaza, promenade, landing, civic-ground, sports-field, playground, and land-use features the later OSM snapshot will contain, and which lack descriptive tags needed for recognizable treatment.
- Exact literal 2026 opening dates and as-built outlines are intentionally no longer layout questions. If a space exists in the frozen snapshot it remains in coverage; if it does not, supplemental sources do not silently add it.

### Views, weather, and material character

**Verified facts**

- The Area Plan identifies Treasure Island's Bay location as unusually windy and says streets and open spaces were planned to preserve views, sunlight, and wind shelter. [S01]
- Official park planning describes the western edge as facing the San Francisco skyline, with the Bay Bridge and Golden Gate Bridge also visible; the southern and eastern edges have the East Bay and the newer Bay Bridge span as backdrops. [S18]
- Cityside Park's accepted current elements include a waterfront public park and the wind-responsive Canopy of Sky artwork. Planning records also identify an event lawn, promenade, stormwater treatment areas, and coastal planting zones in the park. [S08, S19]

**Inferences**

- The approved San Francisco billboard can anchor westward orientation. Clipper Cove, open water, and the simple YBI/Bay Bridge polygon scenery can orient the south/east.
- Wind movement in trees, grasses, flags, water, and Canopy of Sky could be a recognition multiplier, but this is an experiential cue rather than a geometric requirement.
- A plausible material contrast is large areas of legacy asphalt/concrete and utilitarian former-base fabric beside new paving, stormwater planting, park lawns, and contemporary residential construction. The exact palette remains open.

**Uncertainties**

- Which parts of the approved San Francisco billboard are visible from representative third-person traversal positions after snapshot buildings, chosen vegetation, and weather occlude it.
- How legibly the simple YBI and Bay Bridge polygons establish the southern connection from representative traversal positions.
- Road-paving colors and wear, lane striping, curbs, sidewalks, shoreline armoring, vegetation, and temporary construction-material appearance.
- How often fog, haze, wind, and glare materially change recognition at the intended play times.

### Active redevelopment as optional context

**Verified facts**

- Treasure Island is in a multi-phase redevelopment that changes streets, shoreline infrastructure, utilities, parks, and buildings over many years. [S07]
- TIDA's Stage 2 report said main work would begin in June 2025 and run about 36 months to mid-2028. The scope continues Trade Winds and Clipper Cove as through streets; introduces Phillips Lane, Macky Lane, Braghetta Lane, and Peacemakers Street; and includes roadways, sidewalks, lighting, signals, signs, striping, utilities, landscaping, and stormwater gardens. [S05]
- The same report anticipated moving roughly **400,000 cubic yards of soil** and continuing geotechnical work through 2026, useful only as dated context for large-scale earthwork and unfinished-site character. [S05]
- A February 2026 TIDA report said the Treasure Island Road Improvement project was at 55% construction documents, with construction anticipated in Q2 2026 for 18–24 months. It also expected Stage 2 joint-trench work through 2027. [S06]

**Inferences**

- Freezing one latest-at-acquisition OSM extract gives the island layout a coherent source snapshot and avoids mixing a 2010 building layout, 2016 shoreline, 2026 streets, and future master-plan geometry. The frozen snapshot is accepted even if changing reality later differs.
- Construction remains possible appearance/recognition context. Soil piles, equipment, or unfinished-surface dressing are separate optional appearance cues and must not be used to rewrite the frozen layout from later evidence.
- Street records appearing before City acceptance explain why official basemap geometry can lead physical completion or public opening.

**Uncertainties**

- Which construction-like tags or features exist in the later frozen OSM snapshot and what minimal appearance attributes they need.
- Literal August 2026 project progress or openings are intentionally not layout-reconciliation tasks. Dated construction evidence is optional appearance context only.

## Snapshot and supplemental-source controls

The approved layout has one freshness control: record and freeze the latest raw OSM extract available at acquisition/build time. Portal refresh dates and literal real-world completion dates do not override its feature membership or geometry. Freshness remains relevant only to a supplemental attribute or visual claim that is actually used.

Browsed-but-unused candidates do not need a production ledger. This is not a copyright/legal audit.

## Approach-neutral capability implications

These are implications for later work allocation, not choices about how to build the game.

- **Frozen-snapshot ownership is necessary.** One person or agent should record the later OSM extract identity, acquisition time, and Treasure Island bounds, then preserve one immutable layout inventory so contributors do not use different snapshots.
- **Coverage QA is distinct from currentness research.** A geospatial/data reviewer should enumerate every OSM shoreline/landform, road/path, building, public-space, and land-use feature inside the approved boundary and detect extraction omissions or broken relations. They should not compare the snapshot against changing reality as a gate.
- **Simple massing is distinct from layout.** An environment/world reviewer should verify that every frozen OSM building footprint receives a simple exterior mass. Optional OSM tags or contextual evidence may refine only a few recognition-critical silhouettes; missing height/roof attributes do not require per-building research.
- **Traversal validation is a gameplay task.** Representative third-person walk/run loops can be tested across generally traversable outdoor snapshot space. Real fence, private-access, vehicle-rule, and detour research is out of scope; any later restriction is an intentional game/playability boundary.
- **Milestone playability QA has a known target.** A gameplay reviewer should test third-person walk/run and the predefined tag on the owner's MacBook Pro (M1 Pro, 10-core, 32 GB memory) with mouse and keyboard. Camera distance, height, follow behavior, avatar, color, water response, and tag-limit detail should use simple reversible defaults rather than trigger owner questions.
- **Horizon context is fixed at KISS fidelity.** A world/experience reviewer should keep San Francisco as a non-playable billboard and YBI/Bay Bridge as simple non-playable polygonal scenery, all outside the frozen Treasure Island gameplay inventory.
- **Recognition evidence remains distinct and optional.** Public/free landmark evidence can guide simple emphasis while preserving every snapshot feature; whether that emphasis works should be observed naturally in the actual playable, not through a separate owner lab protocol.
- **A local-context pass matters.** Exposition terminal/hangars, Pan Am history, the Navy legacy, street naming, wind, and the old/new transition explain why the island looks the way it does; they are not interchangeable generic waterfront decoration.
- **Visual reference should be targeted, optional, and public/free-source-first.** Use the cited public evidence only for a few high-value landmark/material cues, public-space character, or readability of the one predefined tag—not an exhaustive every-building photo survey, individualized massing study, owner questionnaire, or layout-currentness check.
- **Source dating stays lightweight.** Record the frozen OSM extract and only supplemental references actually shortlisted or integrated, including observation/capture date when known and check date. This is reproducibility, not a rights audit.

## Recognition hypotheses

### Smallest recognition-critical subset inside complete coverage — hypothesis R0

The smallest cue group worth testing is:

1. the broad, very flat, man-made island silhouette with water horizon and a distinct south/southwest connection;
2. the south arrival ensemble of Building 1, the two huge hangars, Clipper Cove, and the ferry landing;

**Inference:** R1 may distinguish an evolving Treasure Island from either a uniformly historic base or a fully built-out future plan. It is an appearance/recognition hypothesis, not a request to reconcile the frozen OSM layout to 2026 construction.
